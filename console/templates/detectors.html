Judgement rate — of the things a person ruled on, the fraction that were wrong. Measures whether the detector is correct. Noise rate — of the observations produced, the fraction that were false. Measures what the detector costs you. They are different questions and the old single rate was named the first while computing the second.

Both denominators are always shown, and a percentage only appears past 20. 2 of 3 refuses the decision that 67% invites — and the judgement denominator is small by design now, so false_positive_rate_rising will report “not computed” until a fortnight has produced five real judgements. That is the honest answer; it never had 45 data points.

The noise rate carries no threshold and no finding yet, deliberately. It is the more actionable number for dd-trustworthy — “this detector costs 45 interruptions per fact” — but nobody knows what a bad value looks like, and picking one now would be inventing it.
